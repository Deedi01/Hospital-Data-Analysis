# Hospital Patient Analytics Dashboard

## Business Scenario
A regional hospital has provided patient records for analysis. While the dataset contains information about patient visits, treatments, costs, and recovery outcomes, management would like a dashboard that summarizes hospital performance and highlights areas requiring attention.

## Business Questions
- Which department treated the highest number of patients? 
- Which Department Head oversees the largest patient volume? 
- Which region recorded the highest number of patient visits? 
- Which treatment type is administered most frequently? 
- Which visit type is most common? 
- Which department has the highest average treatment cost? 
- Which Stay Category is most common? 
- Which Recovery Status occurs most frequently? 
- Which department has the highest average recovery score? 
- Which Budget Category incurred the highest total treatment cost? 
- Which region has the highest number of High Readmission patients? 
- Which Department Head manages the greatest number of Priority Patients? 

## Data
Practice dataset of about 5,000 patient records. Original source unknown.

## Tools
 - **Excel** (Data cleaning, Data enrichment, Visualization)

## Key Metrics
- **Total patients:** 5K
- **Total treatment cost:** 275M
- **Average recovery score:** 74.72
- **Average stay:** 4.06 days
- **Priority patients:** 1,698 (about 40% of all patients)

## Key Findings
- Orthopedics is the busiest and handles the most priority cases, while Neurology is the costliest per patient.
- High patient cost does not necessarily mean better recovery, Neurology cost the most but its recovery scores are not any higher than cheaper departments.
- West and North regions handle the most visits, but East has the highest readmissions. So high patient volume does not mean higher risk.
- 90% of patients recover to "Good" or "Excellent," and only 7% of stays are long-term, suggesting that care is working well overall.
- Routine visits dominate (69%) over emergencies, indicating a largely planned, non-crisis patient load.
